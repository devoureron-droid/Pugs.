The history of the Pug

From imperial Chinese courts to European royal households, Pugs have spent centuries winning people over with their distinctive faces and affectionate nature.

The Pug is a small, sturdy companion breed known for its wrinkled face, short muzzle, curled tail, and smooth glossy coat. Pugs are usually fawn, black, or silver, with a compact body and expressive eyes.

The breed originated in China, where flat-faced companion dogs were treasured by imperial families. Pugs reached Europe in the sixteenth century and became especially popular in the Netherlands through the House of Orange and later in Britain through the House of Stuart.

In the nineteenth century, Queen Victoria developed a passion for Pugs that helped make them fashionable among other members of the British royal family. Their popularity continued into the modern era, helped by their playful, sociable, and adaptable personalities.

Today, Pugs are loved as gentle family companions. Their short muzzle and compact build can also make them vulnerable to breathing, eye, and overheating problems, so thoughtful care and regular veterinary attention are important.
